# Screw as a spring

## Definition

A machine screw under tension behaves like a stiff spring: the shank between the head's bearing surface and the nut's mating face elongates slightly under preload. That elongation is what gives the screw a *range* between loose and fully tight — it's why torque specs make sense and why preload is controllable.

**Corollary**: a **set screw** has no such elongation region. It bottoms out in compression and goes from loose to fully tight in a turn, with no spring range. That's why set screws gouge the material they clamp and can't be fine-tuned.

## How David uses it

Setup in [[videos/borrow-a-tolerance-mindset-for-designers]] — the clamping region of a screw between two surfaces "behaves as a spring" and stretching that region is what lets you get gradually tighter.

Payoff in [[videos/aluminum-extrusions-fundamentals]] — interior set-screw corner brackets are rated lower than exterior angle brackets precisely because the set screw has no spring region; also the reason three-way connectors (set-screw based) are weaker than paired 90° brackets.
