# Emissivity

## Definition

How efficiently a surface emits thermal infrared relative to an ideal blackbody. Shiny metals, glass, and transparent plastics have low or highly variable emissivity — infrared thermometers read *their own reflection* more than the surface temperature.

## How David uses it

In [[videos/more-than-you-ever-wanted-to-know-about-tape]], David shows an IR thermometer reading two different temperatures on the same steel ruler depending on whether it's covered in white duct tape, aluminum foil tape, or left bare — with bare steel deviating the most.

Practical rule he gives: cover the target with **plain beige or white matte masking tape** before taking IR measurements, especially when comparing two surfaces. If the measurement has to be relative rather than absolute, tape both surfaces with the *same* tape so emissivity cancels out. Avoid aluminum foil tape or clear tape for this purpose — they either reflect IR or give you a blended reading.

Note: if you're supplying heat to the target, don't use thick duct tape as the cover — the adhesive insulates and you lose the temperature you're trying to read.
