# 5S methodology

## Definition

Japanese workplace-organization discipline widely adopted in manufacturing and labs. Approximately: sort / set-in-order / shine / standardize / sustain. The observable surface is seemingly-absurd labeling — a chair labeled "chair," a table slot labeled with what goes there — which only makes sense once you've managed enough people that "act without verbal instruction" matters.

## How David uses it

David encountered 5S at [[entities/brands/toyota|Toyota]] during his first internship in Georgetown, Kentucky, and dismissed it as a joke. He now applies it at his [[entities/brands/texas-am|Texas A&M]] lab and says it took "years and years" to appreciate. In [[videos/label-supplies-to-multiply-results]] he frames it as inseparable from his broader philosophy: labels pay back their cost many times over; color coding is a lighter-weight version of 5S that starts self-organizing the lab with minimal effort.

Key practical tells: labels on one face of a box only (backwards = error signal), handwritten labels first and printed only when proven, outdated labels retained as records, QR codes referencing swappable server-side files instead of re-printing.
