## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] presents about 20 small modifications to his closet that eliminate clothes folding entirely. The system: every shirt has a hanger, every hanger has a shirt; pants on dedicated wooden hangers; a galvanized fence-post closet rod; FIFO vertical chutes for socks and small garments; rolled shorts with elastic bands; seasonal fabric bins; a 3D-printed [[entities/tools/hook-bracket|hook bracket]] published on [[entities/brands/grabcad|GrabCAD]]; an automatic light sensor; matte-white walls and a mirror to amplify light; a door sweep so HVAC skips the closet; and Roomba-friendly clear floors.

## Key takeaways

- Replace your closet rod with [[entities/materials/galvanized-fence-post|galvanized fence top rail]] (33.4 mm OD): much stronger than the standard wooden rod, won't bend, and still fits standard hangers.
- FIFO vertical tubes (10-inch [[entities/materials/pvc|PVC]] or even cardboard) for small garments — drop clean ones in the top, pull from the bottom. Strings woven across the bottom keep items from falling through.
- The [[entities/tools/hook-bracket|hook bracket on GrabCAD]] (file name: "hook") is printed in [[entities/materials/abs|ABS]] at 0.3 mm layer / 30% infill and holds ~30 lb. Used to extend hanging space from a side wall.
- Mirror on the closet wall doubles available light by reflecting the ceiling fixture; matte white paint amplifies it further.
- Modify the door so it sweeps near the floor and the HVAC stops conditioning a closet that bakes in direct sunlight — saves a measurable slice of the AC bill.
- Keep the floor clear so a [[entities/tools/roomba|Roomba]] handles sweeping; freeing the floor also makes seasonal fabric bins interchangeable across closets in the home.
- The system thesis: if you want home robots to do useful work, you must first impose enough structure that the task is solvable. Folding-elimination is a step toward robot-readiness.
