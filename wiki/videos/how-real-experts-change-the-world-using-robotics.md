## Overview

Part recruiting call, part progress report on Scuttle Nigeria. David explains that robotics = electrical + mechanical + software engineering (a.k.a. mechatronics) and that any organization that can produce robotics can solve any problem. He then introduces Dr. Gaius Nzebuka at FUTO (Federal University of Technology, Owerri), walks through the Lagos lab's evolution from nothing to a near-complete prototyping setup, and asks the community for one leader to run a fundraiser for a self-built filament extruder.

## Key takeaways

- Robotics = electrical + mechanical + software engineering combined; aerospace and other specialties branch off mechanical. Master those three and you can solve any problem.
- Scuttle has nodes in Texas (2017), Malaysia / Johor Bahru (2020), and now Lagos, Nigeria (2024-2025). A node in Africa closes the time-zone gap so support is available 24 hours.
- The "wrong" approach to robotics organizations is designing your own connectors, batteries, and drivetrains. The right approach is leveraging refined open components (Molex connectors with full datasheets are the example) — vertical non-integration.
- Scuttle Nigeria built a complete prototyping lab from roughly half the standard checkboxes — they even rewired a solar+battery system to give 24-hour power despite intermittent grid power.
- Dr. Nzebuka's team designed a filament extruder (in [[entities/brands/solidworks|SolidWorks]]) that turns plastic pellets into 3D-printer filament — a roughly $15,000 commercial system rebuilt for under a third of that. Sheet-metal bends are spec'd for actual manufacturability.
- The bottleneck isn't capability, it's a leader. David has technicians and engineers in his Discord; what he needs is one person to run a GoFundMe for the extruder parts and stay close to the Nigeria team.
