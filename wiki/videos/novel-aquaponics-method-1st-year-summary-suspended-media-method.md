## Overview

End-of-first-season retrospective on David's backyard aquaponics rig in College Station, Texas. He walks the camera through every subsystem - the glue-free PVC plumbing, perch tank, swirl filter, gravity overflow, suspended media baskets, fishnet enclosure, and timber overhead structure - sharing what worked, what didn't, and the lessons learned over a Texas summer. This is the predecessor to the [[entities/projects/opengrow|OpenGrow]] design.

## Key takeaways

- **Glue-free PVC** worked for the entire season; the ability to disassemble and reroute outweighed the small ongoing leakage at fittings.
- **Single-pump CHOP-style flow** with a swirl filter and gravity overflow self-balances - excess pressure spills harmlessly through an overflow tube, buffering the system against algae fouling and clogs.
- **Six suspended media baskets** in 5-gallon-style buckets favor fruiting plants (tomatoes, bell peppers) over leafy greens, because the dripped water concentrates near the basket center where fruiting roots reach.
- **Expanded clay pellets and expanded shale** as media; clay was preferred for being lightweight when wet, but shale is cheaper if locally sourced (had to drive to Houston for it).
- **Catastrophic flood lesson:** during a heavy rain, groundwater under the partially-buried 100-gallon fish tank made it float and dislodged plumbing - he now routes the DI drain tube far away and downhill.
- **Open-source mindset:** he avoids using found-trash parts because it would break the reproducibility of his design for others.

## Notable observations

- 03:00 - all PVC fittings are glue-free; minor mud seepage but no functional leaks.
- 13:00 - gravity overflow philosophy: oversize incoming pressure, let the surplus dump out an overflow stand-pipe.
- 16:50 - media baskets favor fruiting over leafy plants.
- 25:40 - landscape timbers ($1.75-$2.25 each, later $1 at Walmart) work for non-structural uses despite the "not for structural use" tag.
- 31:00 - the partially-buried tank floated during a heavy rain; root cause was groundwater entering through unpacked fill dirt.
