## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] runs a head-to-head test of seven popular lubricants and protectants on cheap dollar-store rubber bands stretched over a piece of [[entities/materials/pvc|PVC]] pipe. The goal: find out which products attack rubber and which leave it intact, since the marketing claims ("safe on plastics", "rubber conditioner") don't always hold up. Within roughly 10–15 minutes of contact, several products had already snapped the rubber bands.

## Key takeaways

- "Plastic safe" is not the same as "rubber safe" — [[entities/brands/crc|CRC]] 226 is rated safe for [[entities/materials/abs|ABS]], nylon, and Lexan but still snapped rubber bands here.
- The product literally called "3-in-One RV Care Rubber Conditioner" was one of the first to break the rubber, despite being marketed for sealing and conditioning rubber strips.
- [[entities/brands/wd-40|WD-40]] is known to be aggressive on rubber, and David's test confirmed it.
- [[entities/tools/ballistol|Ballistol]] (German recipe, marketed as skin-safe and eco-friendly) and Rem Oil (typically a gun lube) were the gentlest in this set.
- For any project where a lubricant will touch rubber seals, O-rings, or bands, do a small spot test first instead of trusting the label.

## Products tested

- Rem Oil (gentle)
- 3-in-One Oil (regular) — broke rubber
- 3-in-One RV Care Rubber Conditioner — broke rubber first
- [[entities/brands/crc|CRC]] 226 multi-purpose precision lubricant — broke rubber
- [[entities/tools/ballistol|Ballistol]] multi-purpose lubricant / protectant (gentle)
- [[entities/brands/wd-40|WD-40]] — aggressive, expected to break rubber
