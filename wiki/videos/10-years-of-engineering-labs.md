## Overview

David narrates a panoramic tour of labs he has worked in or built across ~10 years — youth education, Scuttle Robotics in Malaysia, metal additive manufacturing, WTA in Brazil, Texas A&M research and teaching labs, and his current OpenLab. His thesis: labs worldwide have converged on the same ~90% subset of tools because mechatronics is now the universal core, and almost everything around us starts on a desk in a lab before reaching production.

## Key takeaways

- A lab is the seminal place — the iPhone, your shoes, every product sits on a lab desk before a production line.
- Across continents and decades the lab tooling has commonized: 3D printers, computers (4 form factors cover 98% — desktop, phone, industrial controller, microcontroller), hand tools, multimeters.
- Cost collapse: a CNC router that was $40,000 a decade ago is $1,000 in kit form today.
- One smart device can replace 5–6 single-purpose instruments; one new tool obviates a drawer full of legacy gear.
- Education ROI: budget that buys one $100k industrial arm buys enough SCUTTLE robots for every two students to dismantle one — and they learn more.
- OpenLab v1 reaches ~90% of the outcomes of all the previous specialized labs combined, with a fraction of the equipment.
- Documentation is a force multiplier — a written, shared lab inventory invites peer critique that no closed lab ever gets.
