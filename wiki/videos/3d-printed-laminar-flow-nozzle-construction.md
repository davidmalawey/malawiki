## Overview

David assembles and seals a 3D-printed laminar flow nozzle, gluing the inlet diffuser to the laminar flow orifice with medium black ABS cement, sealing porosity in the FDM prints with Pro Weld solvent, and adding an [[entities/tools/o-ring|o-ring]] under a clamped acrylic disc for the final waterproof joint. A first water test reveals leaks at the bottom — more glue needed.

## Key takeaways

- 3D prints have inherent porosity between extrusions; a solvent welder like Pro Weld can wick in and seal those gaps after assembly.
- Apply adhesive to both mating surfaces (not for adhesion strength, but to guarantee enough volume to fill the joint), then twist the parts together to spread it.
- A poor sealing surface (e.g. a print overhang) can still be made waterproof by trapping an o-ring against a downstream flat face.
- A cured ABS cement joint hardens to roughly the same hardness as the surrounding plastic — verify with a screwdriver tap.
