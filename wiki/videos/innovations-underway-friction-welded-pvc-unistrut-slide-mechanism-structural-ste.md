## Overview

A wide-ranging [[entities/projects/openlab-project|OpenLab]] progress update from [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] covering several in-flight projects: a salvaged high-pressure water pump, cheap structural [[entities/materials/steel-tubing|steel tubing]] as a primary lab member, [[entities/tools/2020-extrusion|2020 aluminum extrusion]] wall mounting, a rag dispenser and industrial bins, a [[entities/tools/unistrut|Unistrut]] locking-slide mechanism, and modular printable spools. The unifying theme: design around cheap, high-volume, off-the-shelf structural materials and publish parametric brackets on [[entities/brands/grabcad|GrabCAD]] that bind them together.

## Key takeaways

- Cheap structural steel tubing and Unistrut beat aluminum extrusion on cost-per-rigidity for most lab-scale structures; reserve the [[entities/tools/2020-extrusion|2020 extrusion]] for places that benefit from its slot system.
- A round-tube mount bracket plus a 0-degree reference feature lets you re-use the same printed clamp across many tubes and orientations.
- Unistrut locking mechanism: a printed slide can lock against the lipped channel of Unistrut without adding hardware.
- [[concepts/friction-welding-plastic|Friction welding]] of [[entities/materials/abs|ABS]] to [[entities/materials/pvc|PVC]] — discovered while building modular spools — fuses the two plastics directly with no glue, and tolerates messy cuts.
- Publish brackets to [[entities/brands/grabcad|GrabCAD]] so the audience can re-use them: tube-mount bracket, modular spool, bin chute, Unistrut clip.
- The rag-shoot / industrial-bin design lets you pull rags from the bottom in FIFO order without folding.

## Techniques demonstrated

- [[concepts/friction-welding-plastic|Friction welding plastic]] (ABS to PVC, spinning to fuse)
