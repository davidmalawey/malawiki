## Overview

A commentary / vision piece. [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] highlights two YouTube designers — [[entities/people/joe-bowers|Joe Bowers]] (Node Dojo) and [[entities/people/zack-freedman|Zack Freedman]] — whose work he sees as the future of digital making: [[concepts/parametric-design|parametric]], [[concepts/modularity|modular]], 3D-printable systems that gracefully integrate off-the-shelf hardware. He argues these two minds are independently solving complementary halves of the same problem, and that combined with AI-assisted optimization, this is the workflow design engineers should be moving toward.

## Key takeaways

- [[concepts/parametric-design|Parametric design]] grants flexibility: one well-made component branches into infinite outcomes through user-set parameters.
- [[concepts/borrowing-tolerances|Borrowing tolerances]] from off-the-shelf parts (steel rods, motors, bearings) lets a hobbyist capture the engineering value of mass-produced components inside a custom design.
- [[concepts/modularity|Modularity]] means changing one module without breaking the rest of the assembly — essential for iteration.
- AI is an optimizer, not a problem-definer. The new burden on design engineers is articulating the goal precisely; AI will iterate the geometry to meet it.
- [[entities/projects/scuttle-robot|SCUTTLE]] is David's example of this philosophy in robotics: open, documented, modular, multi-disciplinary, built without external funding.
- Open-source ecosystems (Linux analogy) need collaborators to discover each other; private companies that don't share still don't block progress because smart engineers integrate around them.
