## Overview

Step-by-step crimping tutorial for Anderson Powerwerx-style connectors, made for [[entities/places/texas-am-lab|TAMU MXET]] students. Covers the three terminal sizes (15A / 30A / 45A), 18 AWG wire prep, die selection, terminal orientation, the housing assembly with red/black dovetails, the tug test, and how to undo a mistake. Closes with bonded pairs and the locking-pin trick for keeping mated halves together.

## Key takeaways

- Three terminal sizes: 15A, 30A, 45A. The smallest still carries 15A — more than 18 AWG is rated for, so 15A is the default choice for project-level power transmission.
- Strip 8 mm of insulation. Twist the strands. Slide onto the terminal so the conductor sits at the inside tip of the crimped region; if it protrudes, you stripped too much.
- Match the die to the terminal. The crimper is labeled 15 / 30 / 45 — read it.
- Crimp orientation: tongue *down*, resting on the floor of the die. Squeeze the back end first.
- Housing assembly: tab inside the housing points to the floor; tongue of the connector also points down; slide in flat. Then push behind the metal contact with the included tool until you hear a click. Tug test.
- To remove a mis-installed terminal: insert the tool, lift the metal off the spring, push back slightly, pull out.
- Bonded pairs: connectors come pre-glued in red/black dovetail orientation. Keep them coordinated by reading the letters on the housing — red goes on the right.
- Mating halves of separate pairs can be locked together with a small pin (push with pliers or a pin-fed homemade tool); also comes with insertable pins for unbonded pairs.

## Techniques demonstrated

- 8mm strip + twist for stranded 18 AWG
- Tongue-down die orientation
- Two-pass crimp (back end first, then full)
- Tug test verification
- Terminal removal with the lift tool
- Dovetail housing orientation rule (letters readable, red right)
- Pin-locking mated pairs
