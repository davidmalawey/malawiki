## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] argues that his department at [[entities/brands/texas-am|Texas A&M ETID]] has fallen behind universities in developing countries — specifically Malaysia — at training applied robotics engineers. He shows two video clips from his SCUTTLE Asia teams: a 2025 capital-city robotics gathering with multiple working prototypes, and Team Zamalik's 4WD chili-detection-and-spraying robot. He attributes Malaysia's success to purpose-built lab equipment, modern revision-tracked curriculum, and professors humble enough to study alongside students.

## Key takeaways

- Malaysian undergraduates produced novel working robotic prototypes in under 6 months, with state-government and corporate sponsorship.
- All built on the [[entities/projects/scuttle-robot|SCUTTLE]] platform — same curriculum David introduced at Texas A&M in 2017, accelerated 10x faster in Malaysia despite a 4-year head start in Texas.
- Three pillars for success: (1) purpose-built lab equipment plus accessory measuring/testing/modifying tools, (2) modern, revision-tracked, cloud-based curriculum (not 50-year-old textbooks), (3) professors with humility to learn alongside students and a spirit of collaboration.
- David's claim: ETID has zero professors who have personally designed, built, and tested a robotics machine — yet they ask students to produce exactly that.
- The barrier isn't budget or country wealth — it's attitude, community, and action.
