## Overview

Filmed under a macro lens, David walks through the anatomy of a 2.54mm [[entities/tools/dupont-connector|DuPont connector]] and the correct way to crimp it onto 28-gauge wire. He explains the two pairs of wings (insulation grip, then conductor grip), strips ~5mm of insulation, hooks the wing onto the "shelf" inside the smallest die of a [[entities/tools/dupont-crimper|ratcheting DuPont crimper]], and seats the finished pin into the housing until it clicks and locks.

## Key takeaways

- DuPont anatomy: first (larger) pair of wings grabs insulation; second (smaller) pair grabs bare conductor; the female mating area at the tip must stay clear of any wire or insulation.
- Wire size: 28 AWG is the target for 2.54mm DuPont — verified by which hole on the wire stripper cleanly strips it.
- Strip length is roughly 4-5mm; the bare conductor should land in the second pair of wings, the insulation in the first, and nothing should reach the mating area.
- Use the smallest of the three dies on the front of the ratcheting crimper jaw; "label side up" is the convention for orientation on off-the-shelf crimpers.
- Hang the wing on the internal shelf of the die before crimping — this self-registers the part. Pre-clamp lightly to hold the pin steady before inserting the wire from the back side.
- After crimping, the pin slides into the housing's larger hole (small hole = mating face). Listen for the click — once locked, pulling the wire engages the tab and prevents back-out.
