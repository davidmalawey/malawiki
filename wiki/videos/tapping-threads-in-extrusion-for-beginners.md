## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] walks beginners through tapping the end of a [[entities/tools/2020-extrusion|2020]] or [[entities/tools/3030-extrusion|3030]] aluminum extrusion so it can hold a camera, microphone, light, or other 1/4-20 (or M6 / M8) bracket. He covers tap selection, sighting for parallelism, chip-breaking by reversing, and when to graduate from a hand tap handle to a hex-shank drill tap.

## Key takeaways

- 2020 extrusions are pre-engineered for tapping at the end — the central hole accepts M6 or 1/4-20 cleanly. 3030 extrusions are sized for M8.
- 1/4-20 is the standard thread on cameras and tripods, so a tapped 2020 end becomes a universal mounting boss.
- Use both hands on the tap handle, sight from two sides for parallelism in the first few turns, then let the tap pull itself square.
- When resistance increases, reverse a quarter turn — you'll feel the chip break. Brush chips out periodically; clogged flutes break taps.
- Below 1/4" / M6, switch to a hex-shank drill tap (one-pass: drills + taps in a powered driver) — but fixture the work so the tool stays parallel.
- Tooling for fasteners smaller than M6 needs gentle handling; broken taps in extrusion are hard to recover.

## Techniques demonstrated

- Hand-tapping aluminum extrusion (2020, 3030).
- Chip-break protocol — partial-reverse to fragment chips and clear flutes.
- Power-tapping with a hex-shank drill tap for sub-1/4" threads.
