## Overview

A heartfelt, unscripted reply from [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] to disgruntled comments from tradespeople on his DIY and engineering videos. He argues that an engineer can never outperform a tradesman at his own trade, that tools (including robots) should enhance rather than replace skilled workers, and that crossing into ecology, biology, or trades requires partnership with domain experts — not unilateral disruption.

## Key takeaways

- An engineer with a new gadget may optimize one action in a job, but never the whole job — the tradesman still owns the full reality (codes, retrofit constraints, edge cases).
- Impact drivers didn't make screwdrivers obsolete — experts kept both. That is the model for robotics: tools that augment, not replacements.
- In disciplines without mature standards (3D printing, novel mechatronics) disruption is fair game; in plumbing, electrical, agriculture, and ecology it is not.
- Crossing into biology and ecology demands collaboration with funded experts and governmental oversight — engineers should pass possibilities up, not implement unilaterally.
- Critical comments from tradespeople are valued as crowdsourced checks-and-balances, not adversaries.

## Concepts discussed

- Robots and automation as [[concepts/tools-augment-not-replace|tools that augment rather than replace]] human workers.
- [[concepts/cross-disciplinary-humility|Cross-disciplinary humility]] — engineers must defer to domain experts in trades, biology, ecology, and policy.
