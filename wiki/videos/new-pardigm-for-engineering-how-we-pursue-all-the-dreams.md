## Overview

David walks through ~four years of photos from his Malaysian SCUTTLE collaborators to make a single point: open-source, well-documented engineering with cheap globally-sourced parts produces outcomes that closed Western institutions cannot match. He contrasts the Malaysian team's reach (workshops, mall demos, agricultural deployments, partnered universities, government-aligned funding tracks) against Texas A&M's single classroom serving one cohort per semester from the same documentation. Theme: problems are solved with engineering, not money — and the U.S. has bet on the wrong variable.

## Key takeaways

- Four years, no $100k investment, and Malaysian partners have reached every age group, multiple universities, agriculture, healthcare, palm-plantation logistics, and indoor farming.
- Patents don't matter; documented, parametric, locally-sourced designs do.
- Mobile robotics is the natural next step after smartphone automation — phone is computer + Wi-Fi + display + memory; just attach wheels and motor control.
- SCUTTLE base software now has 16 forks; teams can swap chassis, drivetrain, and payload while keeping the documented power scheme and electronics architecture.
- A degree isn't required — passionate technicians who add value beat passive engineers who don't.
- Nigeria deployment is next; David expects results to exceed Malaysia because resources are scarcer and motivation is higher.
- Engineering is culture-agnostic — international collaboration was always how it was supposed to work.
