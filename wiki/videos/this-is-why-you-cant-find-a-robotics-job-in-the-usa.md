## Overview

A long-form opinion piece. [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] uses Malaysia and Singapore — where he lived and worked through 2020 — as a counter-example to U.S. industrial policy: tightly coordinated government technology investment, fund-matching programs, and dedicated buildings produce a robotics jobs market that the U.S. simply doesn't have. He argues American robotics graduates can't find robotics work at home because the government structure that would create those jobs doesn't exist.

## Key takeaways

- Singapore concentrates so much technology investment that the overflow seeds a robotics industry across the causeway in Johor, Malaysia — visible to anyone living there but invisible to the U.S. media diet.
- Government-backed fund matching, dedicated tech-park buildings, and explicit workforce-pipeline budgets are levers other governments pull and the U.S. largely doesn't.
- The U.S. mental model — "the government can't just throw money at something and make it exist" — is correct in isolation but wrong as a reason to do nothing; coordinated programs are what stand up an industry.
- David frames his own work ([[entities/projects/scuttle-robot|SCUTTLE]], the [[entities/projects/scuttle-robotics-lab-nigeria|Nigeria lab]]) as filling the gap that U.S. policy leaves open.
