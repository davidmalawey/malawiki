## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] introduces the "Venn bracket" (a.k.a. "bikini bracket") — a small geometric shape that occupies the negative space between three tangent circles. He invites the open-source maker community to play with it because PVC and ABS bond strongly with cheap PVC cement, making this a low-cost basis for modular assemblies. His Texas A&M colleague [[entities/people/angel-paredes|Angel]] joins to riff on the geometry and explore alternative arrangements.

## Key takeaways

- The shape fills the gap where three circles (or PVC tubes) meet tangentially — David calls it "the second most useful shape after a circle."
- ABS-printed brackets bond permanently to [[entities/materials/pvc|PVC]] tube using standard PVC cement, exploiting strong polymer compatibility and very low cost.
- Six brackets around a central tube produce a honeycomb (60° spacing); Angel proposes a variant with channels every 30° to also support 4-circle (90°) layouts.
- 60 mm bracket height (3 × 20 mm vertical units) lets you stack attachments and still mate three brackets in a triangle.
- Angel suggests adding a Lego-style pin/hole feature so the brackets self-locate and stack in 3D.
- The design is fully open source on [[entities/brands/grabcad|GrabCAD]] under "bikini_bracket"; the spirit is "the design belongs to the world."

## Techniques demonstrated

- [[concepts/borrowing-tolerances|Borrowing tolerances]] — using existing PVC pipe diameters as the tolerance baseline.
- [[concepts/parametric-design|Parametric design]] — iterating bracket dimensions to fit different angle arrangements.
- Open-source design release as an "Easter egg" for the community to discover and improve.
