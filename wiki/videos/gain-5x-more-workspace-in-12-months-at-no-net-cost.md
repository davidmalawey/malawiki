## Overview

A direct sequel to the "200% output" mindset video. [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] lays out a multi-iteration method — driven by blue tape and red tags — to triage what actually deserves prime workspace real estate. Iteration one (30 days) removes ~50% of items by time-based usage; iteration two (90 days) trims further; a one-year sealed-box rule clears the long tail; and finally a $20-per-cubic-foot value test moves overflow to "Lowe's becomes my shelves." Side themes: cash-swap psychology (literally setting aside $24 to prove you can replace what you discard), temperature-tolerance color coding, and the "let-it-simmer" mental practice for naming items that have no obvious home.

## Key takeaways

- Treat your workspace like a city: prime arms-reach real estate must be productive 100% of the time. Trinkets and low-value items go up on a shelf.
- Tape every box; remove the tape when the box is opened during the week. After 30 days, untaped boxes are non-essential — half your space.
- Tier the time test: 30 → 60 → 90 days, then a 1-year sealed-box rule (don't even open it; trash it).
- Initial fill: never occupy more than 50–60% of any new bin. Flexibility is required to relocate misclassified items as you learn the system.
- Color tags can carry temperature data: red = "can't go hot," blue = "can't freeze." Two lost lubricants is a fair price for not having to read every data sheet again.
- Cash-swap technique: pull $24 cash, label an envelope "glitter and paint," file it. The decision is made. If you change your mind, the store is your storage — they keep it climate-controlled and labeled for free.
- Below ~$20/cubic-foot of inconvenient real estate, donate or discard rather than store.
- Industrial-grade organizers (pegboards, hinged panels, 2020 extrusion) hold their value for a decade — buy with that horizon.
- Don't wait for "creativity": creativity emerges from the act of prioritizing the problem and treating your own friction as legitimate.

## Techniques demonstrated

- Blue-tape "first 30 days" purge (~50% reduction).
- Red-tag system (originally from industry; refined into temperature-tolerance and cash-swap layers).
- 1-year sealed-box rule — close, tape, date; if seal isn't broken in 12 months, it never opens.
- Cash-swap psychological tool — physically setting aside replacement cost to commit to a discard.
- Hinged pegboards on the wall to multiply storage planes (back side becomes file storage / manual storage).
- Combining 2020 extrusion + pegboard for new mounting types.
