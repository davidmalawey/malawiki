## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] argues that student and capstone teams overload themselves by reinventing every aspect of a robot. Instead, he proposes treating mobile robots as mobile sensors: borrow the robot platform (e.g. [[entities/projects/scuttle-robot|SCUTTLE]]), drop a small wireless IoT sensor cluster on top, and ship a real, valuable system in a single semester.

## Key takeaways

- Wireless IoT sensors (humidity, barometric pressure, IR temperature, ambient/UV light, motion) all fit in a small 3D printed enclosure and are within reach of any undergraduate.
- Mobile phones already proved the "mobile sensor" paradigm — Google Maps traffic is just sensors going where people go. Mobility lets you place sensors by GPS or by criteria the robot itself is sensing.
- Capstone teams should constrain effort to their existing skill set. A Python/AI student should write the software, not redesign the chassis.
- A simpler scoping move (add sensor, don't rebuild robot) shifts most of the work to writing software, GUIs, and B2B applications — far more commercializable.
- This is the philosophy behind [[entities/projects/scuttle-robot|SCUTTLE]]: borrowed, documented, free-to-repeat hardware so teams can focus where they add value.

## Techniques demonstrated

- Project-scoping by skill set: choose deliverables that match the team's strongest discipline.
- Treating an open-source robot as a platform substrate rather than a re-invention exercise.
