## Overview

A 3-minute tutorial for MXET students on using a universal/round-cable stripper to remove the outer jacket of round cables (coax, Ethernet, 120 V power, USB) without nicking the inner conductor insulation. David shows the depth-adjustment routine, the ring-cut technique, and when to switch to a traditional gauge stripper for the inner wires.

## Key takeaways

- A universal round-cable stripper has two functions: a back-end wire cutter for clean perpendicular cuts, and a front-end ring stripper with an adjustable blade depth.
- Set blade depth empirically: lower the blade until the cable barely won't fit, then raise the cable through, then drop the blade back ~180 degrees — adjust by ~90-degree increments.
- The technique works on coax, Ethernet, USB, and 120 V mains cables — anywhere you need to keep inner conductor insulation intact.
- After ring-cutting the jacket, bend the cable to confirm the cut is fully around, then peel it off by fingernail.
- For the inner wires, switch to a traditional gauge stripper or use the strip dies built into the same tool.
- For shielded cables (e.g. USB), peel the foil/braid shield separately to expose the power conductors.

## Techniques demonstrated

- Depth-tuning a round-cable stripper by feel.
- Ring cut + peel to remove jacket without conductor damage.
- Step-up to a gauge-specific stripper for inner conductors.
- [[concepts/strain-relief|Strain relief]] preservation by leaving inner insulation intact.
