## Overview

Short SCUTTLE-build clip showing how to prep the 3D-printed wheel bracket's axle hole right after it comes off the printer. The plastic holes print undersized (around 7.91-8.00 mm) and need to be cleaned out with an 8.0 mm metric drill bit so the axle slides through without engaging the threads against the plastic. David closes with a memorable analogy comparing proper hole fit to a properly fitting motorcycle helmet.

## Key takeaways

- 3D-printed axle holes on the [[entities/projects/scuttle-robot|SCUTTLE]] wheel bracket (v1.3) come out roughly 7.93-8.00 mm and resist the axle.
- Use an 8.0 mm metric [[entities/tools/drill-bit|drill bit]] (not imperial) to chase the hole — do not let the threads cut into the plastic.
- Threads are not supposed to engage the bracket. The hole should be just barely larger than the bolt shank.
- An oversized hole concentrates load on a small contact patch, creating a stress concentration. A correctly sized hole spreads the force over a larger area.
- Same physics as helmet fit: a too-loose helmet concentrates impact at a single point and can break the skull.
