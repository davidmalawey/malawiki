## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] tells the story of the [[entities/places/misl-lab|MISL lab]] (Multidisciplinary Integrated Systems Lab) at [[entities/brands/texas-am|Texas A&M]], run by his advisor [[entities/people/joseph-morgan|Dr. Joseph Morgan]] from before 2012 until his 2021 retirement. He treats the lab as a case study for how to sustainably run a hands-on undergraduate engineering lab — covering student pay, equipment management, knowledge transfer, building access, and the relationship with sponsoring outside businesses. The intended audience is educators (like [[entities/people/gozie-nzebuka|Dr. Nzebuka]] in Nigeria, mentioned at the start) trying to spin up similar programs.

## Key takeaways

- A successful multidisciplinary lab needs at least one faculty member who can both build hands-on engineering devices AND interface with outside businesses on sponsored projects.
- The MISL/PIC structure: ~5–6 ongoing projects, ~3 per semester, ~10 students rotating in and out, students self-selecting by knocking on the advisor's door.
- Lab continuity across semesters enables [[concepts/kaizen|kaizen]] (continuous improvement) — projects don't reset every term.
- Modular table spaces with whiteboards and dropped power let multiple teams work in parallel; a separate noisy-mechanical room handles loud cuts and filings.
- A weekly capstone-style report-out keeps the technical communication standards high.
- Outside companies sponsor projects in this lab (instead of capstone) when they want longer-than-one-semester engagement and student-research-grade robustness.
- Students get paid, equipment gets maintained, and trust scales with student-led management — this only works because rules and norms accumulated over a decade.
