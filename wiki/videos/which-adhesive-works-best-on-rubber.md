## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]]'s original adhesive-on-[[entities/materials/rubber|rubber]] test, filmed in [[entities/places/malaysia|Malaysia]] from glues bought at a local Mr. DIY store. He bonds rubber pairs with batteries as compression weights for ~12 hours, then peel-tests four candidates — "magic glue," a contact adhesive, a "german glue," and a shoe adhesive (the previous best). None are as strong as he wants, so the round ends without a true winner and he sets up a [[videos/which-adhesive-works-best-on-rubber-round-2-one-winner|round 2]] with construction adhesive, clear RTV, more shoe adhesive, and red high-temp gasket maker. This is the predecessor to the more polished 2024 [[videos/how-to-choose-an-adhesive|adhesive selection]] video.

## Key takeaways

- [[entities/materials/rubber|Rubber]] is genuinely hard to bond — David openly says he has never really succeeded.
- Pre-drying contact adhesive longer (5 minutes vs immediate) improved strength noticeably.
- The exposed (air-cured) edges of the contact adhesive bond were stronger than the buried interior — air contact matters for that chemistry.
- A 12-hour cure was used; David suspects another day would have helped some of the bonds.
- Channel positioning: David explicitly calls this a "lessons archive" rather than a production studio — the [[concepts/share-the-failure|share-the-failure]] mode that defines the channel.

## Techniques demonstrated

- [[concepts/adhesive-selection-method|Adhesive selection method]] — set up matched samples, weight to compress, cure overnight, peel-test.
- [[concepts/peel-vs-shear|Peel vs shear]] testing as the failure mode of interest for rubber.
- Using AA / 18650 batteries as compression weights during cure.

## Tools used

- AA / [[entities/tools/18650-cell|18650]] batteries used as compression weights.

## Materials used

- [[entities/materials/rubber|Rubber]] — the substrate being bonded.

- Shoe adhesive (Daiso, Japanese) — previously the best; decent but underwhelming this round.
- "German glue" — a loser.
