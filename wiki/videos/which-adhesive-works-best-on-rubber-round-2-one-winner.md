## Overview

Sequel to [[videos/which-adhesive-works-best-on-rubber|round 1]]. [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] cures seven adhesives on [[entities/materials/rubber|rubber]] for ~18-36 hours and peel-tests them. Six fail (hot glue, high-temp RTV, construction adhesive, clear silicone RTV, two-part epoxy, quick-grip contact adhesive); only JSL [[entities/materials/super-glue|cyanoacrylate super glue]] holds — bonded so well the rubber tore before the joint did, the canonical "stronger than the substrate" outcome. This becomes a reference result the channel returns to in later adhesive videos.

## Key takeaways

- **Winner: [[entities/materials/super-glue|cyanoacrylate (super glue)]]** — the only adhesive in the round strong enough to tear the rubber rather than peel cleanly.
- Hot glue: peels off both surfaces, do not use on rubber.
- High-temperature RTV: did not bond to rubber.
- Construction adhesive: bonded to both surfaces but delaminated easily; David speculates an [[entities/materials/acetone|acetone]] or [[entities/materials/isopropyl-alcohol|alcohol]] surface prep might have helped.
- Clear silicone RTV (advertised as 100% silicone rubber): peels off too easily despite the marketing.
- Two-part epoxy: too rigid to flex with rubber, very poor bond — but interestingly the thin epoxy layer itself didn't crack, only failed at the interface.
- Quick-grip contact adhesive: yellowish, no better than the others.
- Cure time matters: most packages claim 24h full cure; David tested at 18-36h.

## Techniques demonstrated

- [[concepts/adhesive-selection-method|Adhesive selection method]] — head-to-head peel testing under controlled cure time and weight.
- [[concepts/peel-vs-shear|Peel vs shear]] — peel tests as the binding failure mode for shoe-repair-style joints.
- [[concepts/surface-preparation|Surface preparation]] — David flags that solvent prep might rescue some of the failures, hinting at a future test.
- "Stronger than the substrate" as the success bar for adhesive selection on rubber.
