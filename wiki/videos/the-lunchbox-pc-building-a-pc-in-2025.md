## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] tours [[entities/people/angel-paredes|Angel]]'s newly finished compact ITX gaming PC ("the lunchbox") — an [[entities/brands/asus|ASUS]] B650E-I motherboard, AMD CPU, AMD 7900 GRE GPU, and a [[entities/brands/noctua|Noctua]]-fanned all-copper heat-pipe cooler under $40. They run a thermal-camera comparison, demo Forza 5 and Hogwarts Legacy at 4K, debate DDR4 vs. DDR5, and David shares the story of his lost PC in Korea. The episode functions as a community Q&A on PC-building in 2025.

## Key takeaways

- ITX small-form-factor builds are now competitive with full ATX on cost and capability — the constraint that previously forced exotic-priced parts has eased.
- Modern heat-pipe coolers move heat by capillary phase change inside copper tubes — high performance for under $40.
- The 7900 GRE (two-fan variant) is the largest GPU that fits this case while delivering near-flagship performance.
- Audience poll: 44% of viewers had built a PC within 3 years; 24% over 8 years ago.
- Recommend DDR5 over DDR4 in 2025 — DDR4 is being phased out.
- A thermal camera is a fast diagnostic for verifying airflow and component health.

## Techniques demonstrated

- [[concepts/heat-pipe-cooling|Heat-pipe cooling]] — phase-change capillary loop inside copper tubes for passive heat transport.
- Thermal-camera inspection of intake/exhaust temperatures.
- ITX component selection (matching GPU length, cooler height, PSU shroud to a small case).
