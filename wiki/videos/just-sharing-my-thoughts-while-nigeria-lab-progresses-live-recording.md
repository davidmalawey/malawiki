## Overview

A reflective, semi-stream-of-consciousness recording. While Dr. Nzebuka's team in Nigeria is building a 3D-printer extruder for the [[entities/projects/scuttle-robotics-lab-nigeria|SCUTTLE Robotics Lab — Nigeria]], [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] thinks out loud about how to fundraise transparently from his YouTube audience, why a robotics-trained student pipeline is the engine of national economic growth, and how [[entities/projects/scuttle-robot|SCUTTLE Robotics]] is meant to be a replicable lab template for any university anywhere.

## Key takeaways

- The Nigeria lab is a working test of whether an open-source mechatronics lab template can be stood up remotely, with engineers (not marketing or comms staff) running fundraising directly.
- Engineers leading the work means the GoFundMe / donation pitch is delivered without a polished marketing layer — the trade-off is authenticity for reach.
- Dr. Nzebuka's students are using [[entities/brands/solidworks|SolidWorks]] (newly licensed at their university) to design sheet-metal enclosures and lead fabrication themselves — the lab becomes a training ground, not just a build site.
- Each lab is meant to "output more value than it costs" by graduating engineering-literate students back into their economies.
- David's preferred fundraising posture: per-milestone bullet points showing exactly what the money bought, so trust accrues to the project even when costs (e.g. shipping) overrun expectations.
- The project intentionally avoids one-shot $10k donor fixes from connected friends, because building the funding muscle through the public audience is what makes the model replicable for the next lab (e.g. a Canadian university viewer).
