## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] demonstrates a 2-year-tested DIY shoe-insole design: trace a template onto self-adhesive [[entities/materials/cork|natural cork]] sheet, laminate the back with [[entities/materials/duck-cloth|cotton duck cloth]] to cover the adhesive, and cut to shape with a fresh blade on a cutting mat. Pair cost lands around $2.40 against ~$20 for the bestselling petroleum-plastic insole on Amazon.

## Key takeaways

- Natural cork is naturally antimicrobial and antifungal, conforms to the shoe within ~1.5 days of wear, and outlasts the fabric layer of most store-bought inserts.
- Geometry is forgiving: only three curves matter (forefoot circle, heel circle, gentle arch line). The same template fits multiple shoe brands.
- Two insoles fit on one 12-inch square cork sheet — almost zero waste if you trim the flat edge first and trace flush.
- Wax the blade (beeswax) to keep the adhesive layer from gumming the edge.
- Make extra pairs and rotate them; UV sunlight kills bacteria better than any spray.
- David's "things are free now" thesis: a $20 product loses to a $2.40 DIY equivalent that lasts twice as long.

## Techniques demonstrated

- [[concepts/laminate-cloth-to-cork|Laminating cloth to a self-adhesive cork sheet]] using a roller
- Tracing a saved digital template (PDF) so worn-in curl doesn't corrupt the next trace
- Inverting the template for left vs right (the shape isn't symmetric)
