## Overview

An unscripted call-to-action: David argues that the public discourse on robotics and AI is being dominated by marketers, entertainers, and tech YouTubers while subject-matter experts stay silent. He asks fellow engineers, founders, and researchers to start producing raw, imperfect commentary — because the dialogue gap is steering funding, careers, and policy in the wrong direction.

## Key takeaways

- Public conversation about robotics/AI is loud but expert input is nearly absent — the people with the depth aren't producing content.
- The Kansas City vs. Kansas analogy: the audience is "almost right but pointed the wrong way," and a small expert nudge can redirect huge resources.
- Engineers hesitate because they fear imperfect opinions or low engagement; David argues that being wrong is fine — experts adapt fast when challenged.
- Boston Dynamics Atlas can carry a firearm but cannot cast a fishing pole; a 5-year-old can. Most humanoid robots have nearly zero true Dynamics. Public discourse should be calling this out.
- An iPhone is already a video studio. The bar to participate is low. Just talk.
