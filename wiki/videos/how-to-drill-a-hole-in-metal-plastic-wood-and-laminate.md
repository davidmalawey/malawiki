## Overview

A 45-minute reference compendium on hole-making across materials, dedicated to "the PhD folks" — robotics researchers who build prototypes but may have skipped fundamentals. [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] cycles through drill guides, center punches, depth control, concentricity, twist drills, step drills, Forstner bits, spade bits, deburring tools, runout, terminology, holes-in-slots, small holes, materials-specific tactics for steel and plastic, and finishes with a press-fit experiment. The video is built around strategies, not exhaustive material/tool coverage.

## Key takeaways

- A drill guide enforces perpendicularity (impossible to eyeball reliably) and forces you to commonize on standard hole sizes that match available hardware.
- An automatic center punch with adjustable force prevents drill walking and avoids splitting weak materials.
- For depth control, set-screw collars, custom HDPE stops, or even doubled O-rings work — it depends how much axial-force control you have.
- A twist drill self-aligns via long contact points; a Forstner has a small moment arm so it can wander unless held steady.
- Step drill bits are the cleanest way through thin material; Forstner bits handle irregular/curved surfaces; spade bits are fastest in wood but lowest precision.
- For small holes (<1/8"), three-jaw chucks lose grip — switch to [[entities/tools/collet|collet]]-based tools like a Dremel.
- Steel demands lubrication (tapping fluid, WD40, or beeswax-thickened paste) plus carbide or cobalt for hard or abrasive materials; chip shape (fine dust vs. shavings) tells you whether torque/pressure is right.
- 0.1 mm oversize is too much for a press-fit into a hardened pin — the pin shears the hole.
- A drill bit itself can serve as a pin gauge to check finished hole size.

## Techniques demonstrated

- [[concepts/borrowing-tolerances|Borrowing tolerances]] (commonizing hole sizes against available hardware).
- Center-punching to prevent drill walking.
- Backing thin materials with sacrificial stock and clamping to prevent spin-up injuries.
- Concentric-hole layout with a 45° square as a centering jig.
- Enlarging a hole into a slot using a conical bit.
- Deburring with a drum tool sized at ~½ the hole diameter.
- Press-fit hole sizing experiment (3.6 mm pin into 3.5 mm hole — fails).
