## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] tells the story of how a three-column, ten-row spreadsheet comparing campus gym square footage across major Texas universities — sent to the dean's office and never replied to — likely contributed to [[entities/places/texas-am|Texas A&M]] quietly building two new gymnasiums by the time he returned from Asia. He then unpacks why this works: truthful data + simple format + an organization that has published its core values + a society that cares about alignment between values and actions. The tool is free, available to anyone, and overrides credentials.

## Key takeaways

- A truthful, repeatable, simple spreadsheet — even a third of a page — can shift institutional behavior when it threatens to expose a gap between published core values and actual decisions.
- The authority is not the messenger; it's the data and the published values. "It's not me — I don't carry the authority. The truth carries the authority."
- Why staff and professors don't speak up: each person above the bottom rung has something to lose. The bottom rung carries the duty *and* the freedom.
- Decisions at the top get made on filtered information from people who don't share the demographic's reality (parking, working a job while studying, no money for off-campus gym).
- Published core values matter — they are the public's hook for accountability, even decades after they were written.
