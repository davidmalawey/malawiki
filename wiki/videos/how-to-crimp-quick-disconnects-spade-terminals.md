## Overview

A short tutorial on using a Gardner Bender GS-388 crimper to install spade-terminal quick disconnects on stranded wire. David covers when to use them, the two common sizes, how to strip and seat the wire, which die to use, and how to crimp doubled-up 18-gauge leads in a single blue terminal.

## Key takeaways

- Quick disconnects are most common in AC household appliances where wires don't get cycled often.
- Pink terminals fit up to 16 AWG; blue terminals fit up to 14 AWG. David's projects standardize on 18 AWG.
- Strip 5-10 mm of insulation, twist the strands, and seat the wire so the insulation reaches the inner shelf of the terminal sleeve.
- Always do the tug test after crimping to confirm the wire is captured.
- Orient the crush dimple at 12 or 6 o'clock — never sideways — or the terminal won't seat fully on the spade.
- Two 18 AWG wires can share one blue terminal for junction-style splits, but 18 AWG only supports up to ~16 amps total.

## Techniques demonstrated

- [[concepts/strain-relief|Strain relief]] — seating insulation under the terminal's outer crimp shelf.
- Tug-test verification of crimped joints.
- Sharing two wires in a single oversized terminal as an in-line junction.
