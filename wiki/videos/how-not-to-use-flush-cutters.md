## Overview

A 2-minute "yes/no" rapid-fire video aimed at Aggie senior engineering students: which materials should and should not be cut with [[entities/tools/flush-cutters|flush cutters]] (an Eigen 330 with thick jaws). David demonstrates by trying each item and stating "yes" or "no" — copper wire yes, steel wire no, Aggie ring no, magnet test included.

## Key takeaways

- Flush cutters are designed for soft, ductile metals. Cutting hardened steel will nick or chip the jaws.
- Yes list: copper wire, zip ties, aluminum wire, wood, nickel-clad copper, header pins, stranded copper, through-hole PCB pins, the Texas Longhorn.
- No list: steel wire, Allen keys, staples, washers, spring steel, screws, the Aggie Ring.
- Quick test if you forget: if a magnet picks it up, it likely contains steel — don't cut it.
- This is part of a small how-to series for Aggie engineering seniors and pairs with the ferrule-crimping tutorial released the next day.
