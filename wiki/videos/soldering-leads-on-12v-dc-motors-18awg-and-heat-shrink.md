## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] walks through the full procedure for soldering 18 AWG power leads to a 12 V DC gearmotor for [[entities/projects/scuttle-robot|SCUTTLE]]: flux the contact, pre-thread the heat shrink, mechanically crimp the wire through the terminal eye, then flow plenty of solder so it saturates the strands. The video highlights the most common rookie mistake - forgetting to slip heat shrink on before soldering.

## Key takeaways

- Apply rosin/flux to the motor's terminal contact before anything else.
- Strip about 10 mm of insulation off the 18 AWG wire and gently twist the strands.
- Match red wire to the motor's positive terminal.
- Pass the wire through the terminal eye, bend it back, and compress so you don't end up with a fat ball.
- Slide heat shrink on the wire before soldering and stand it back from the joint so radiated heat doesn't shrink it prematurely.
- Bend the conductor at the midpoint of the stripped section so it doesn't double up over the insulation.
- Re-flux the wire itself just before soldering for fast solder flow and minimal insulation melting.
- Wait for the joint to cool before sliding heat shrink down so it doesn't shrink onto bare solder.
- Heat shrink covers top and bottom faces of the joint; the finished motor lead has no exposed conductor.
