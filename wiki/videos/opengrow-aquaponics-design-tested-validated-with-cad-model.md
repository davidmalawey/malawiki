## Overview

A short walkthrough of David's open-source backyard aquaponics rig (results from 2018), with a published [[entities/brands/grabcad|GrabCAD]] model. The system's defining engineering choice is that all water levels across all subsystems are engineered so a **single pump** moves water through the whole loop. The design also leverages natural sunlight (no LED supplementation), passive cooling from water-fall paths and an earth-embedded fish tank, and is sized for backyard use but scales up.

## Key takeaways

- One pump for the entire loop — water levels at each subsystem are designed so gravity does the rest. This is unusual for aquaponics.
- Natural sunlight, not LEDs. Most well-designed aquaponic systems supplement with LEDs or go fully indoor; OpenGrow's appeal is the LED savings, especially attractive for climates like [[entities/places/malaysia|Malaysia]].
- Pollinators can access the system (it's open-air), enabling fruiting plants — combined with sunlight access and multi-season operation, this puts it in a small set of aquaponics configurations.
- Fish waste from the tank produced more nutrients than expected. Only micronutrient supplementation needed (e.g., potassium).
- Roots survived light exposure — validated experimentally.
- Passive cooling: the water's fall path provides cooling for free; the fish tank is embedded in the earth and back-filled with dirt/gravel for thermal mass.
- A swirl filter handles solids; capacity and effectiveness validated experimentally.
- Construction: cinder blocks and wood structure, gravel base for drainage — heavier than strictly required for backyard scale, but engineered for longevity and as a template that scales.
