## Overview

A short, unscripted essay where David tries — for the first time, in his words — to articulate what he is actually trying to do with [[entities/projects/scuttle-robot|SCUTTLE]]: commoditize custom engineering solutions. Software has been open-source and modular for 20 years. Now hardware can be too, via 3D printing plus off-the-shelf commodity parts plus free education plus translation, putting every "ingredient" of a custom solution into the free or low-cost column.

## Key takeaways

- Commoditization is not "make it cheap" — it is making the ingredients (software, designs, hardware modules, education, translation) so freely available that nobody can charge more than the thing is actually worth.
- High schoolers and inexperienced engineers can already produce advanced solutions by copying and pasting modules; this is the explicit goal, not a side effect.
- SCUTTLE plus accessories covers ~80% of the engineering solutions that existed at the dawn of the automobile age — once you can build a robot, you can build almost anything.
- The four ingredients commoditized: mechanical design (free via 3D printing), electronics/circuit boards (already free), off-the-shelf hardware (commodity), and education (YouTube, PDFs, free books).
- Big corporations and experienced manufacturers explicitly do not design for 3D printing — David sees that gap as the differentiator, not a problem.
- Real-time machine translation makes language no longer a barrier — the whole package becomes globally accessible.
