## Overview

A long-form commentary essay. [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] uses the Swiffer mop as a worked example of hardware "enshittification" — products that were ostensibly engineered for users now optimize for shareholders. He proposes two reframes (an *invention-maturity diagram* and a *product-for-stakeholders* model), connects it to his [[entities/brands/toyota|Toyota]] localization-engineering experience, and argues that any individual designer can beat the bestseller by combining a better off-the-shelf component (his [[entities/tools/doodlebug-mop|3M Doodlebug]] industrial pad) with the *training* that the bestseller deliberately omits.

## Key takeaways

- Once a product holds a market-leader position in a steady-size market, the design team's goals shift from user value to cost reduction — wall thickness shrinks, fiber count drops, alloys change to whatever passes a minimum-stress test.
- The Swiffer's market dominance is reinforced by serving a generation of mobile, time-pressured millennials who buy temporary, training-free, intuitively-packaged products.
- The [[entities/tools/cuban-mop|stick / Cuban mop]] sits a few feet away on the same Walmart shelf with no labels, no instructions, no how-to — Walmart can't *not* stock it (some customers already know it), but they make sure no Swiffer customer accidentally adopts it.
- The general formula a corporation withholds: *product + training = solution*. A YouTube-published method becomes the missing training and effectively unlocks the niche.
- David's [[entities/projects/doodlebug-mop-build|Doodlebug-based mop]] uses an industrial pad holder, industrial hook-and-loop, and a [[entities/tools/scotchbrite|Scotchbrite]]-family scouring pad with embedded ceramic particles. Documented as a parts list on the [[entities/projects/openlab-project|OpenLab]] page.
- Toyota localization story: as an engine designer at the [[entities/places/toyota-georgetown-kentucky|Toyota Georgetown plant]], David's job was to enable multi-source supply across US/Japan suppliers — instructive about how corporate goals diverge from end-user value.
- Reframe trick for inventors: treat the apparent leading product as a *niche*, then look for the historically or geographically dominant solution somewhere else in the world.
