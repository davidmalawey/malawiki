## Overview

A 40-minute live unboxing and first-print of the [[entities/tools/flashforge-creator-pro2|Flashforge Creator Pro 2]] — an IDEX (Independent Dual Extruders) FDM machine. David assembles the extruders, threads filament, levels the bed, installs FlashPrint, and kicks off two simultaneous Benchy prints. Along the way he riffs on poka-yoke design, anti-ooze plates, brass nozzle internal geometry, and why FlashForge is underrated in the hobbyist community.

## Key takeaways

- Creator Pro 2's IDEX lets both extruders print independent parts simultaneously — major productivity win for a SCUTTLE production line.
- Steel frame and enclosure make ABS practical right out of the box (where the cheap [[entities/brands/creality|Creality]] Ender forces PETG).
- Allen-wrench short ends serve as built-in torque limiters: if your finger hurts pressing, that's about right for M3 hardware.
- Asymmetric extruder mounts are a [[concepts/poka-yoke|poka-yoke]] — physically impossible to install on the wrong side.
- The "anti-ooze plate" caps the inactive nozzle when the active one prints — a tested mechanical solution that no amount of FEA could have predicted blind.
- A Wisconsin-Madison researcher's nozzle-geometry study suggests flat internal lands outperform standard twist-drilled nozzles, but production economics force the simpler shape.
- David prefers SD-card workflows over Wi-Fi for reliability — bells and whistles aren't a substitute for repeatability.
- ABS chosen over PLA/PETG in Malaysia's humid environment because ABS doesn't absorb moisture and degrades far less.
