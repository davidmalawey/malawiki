## Overview

A personal essay made for a friend who is stuck — depression, anxiety, doubt about a career path she nominally believed in. David offers the metaphor of a rope and anchor: when you can't push yourself forward, you need a goal that *pulls* you back toward your path no matter which direction you sidestep into. He then walks through a values → guides → activities mapping exercise he stumbled into during his own hard years, designed to expose whether the work you do today is actually fed by the values you hold.

## Key takeaways

- Side-stepping is exhausting. Cooking, gym, friends, romance, study — each is a valid value, but they pull in different directions and you lose energy diverging more than progressing.
- The anchor is a goal worth pursuing. The "you in January" cannot trick the "you in March" — if the goal isn't truly worthwhile to your unchanging core, you'll find out eventually.
- "Tell me who you are without saying your gender, age, or occupation." Two auto mechanics put in the same societal box can be totally different people pursuing totally different reasons.
- The exercise: write 3 values (kept simple — generosity, knowledge growth, efficiency in his example), break each into 2-3 specific definitions ("give to strangers," "learn every month," "don't waste fuel"), then cross-pollinate to derive **guides** (e.g., generosity × teach-as-you-learn = "publication monthly educational"), then map current activities back to the values via the guides.
- A **rule** punishes you when broken; a **guide** is a rule with the failure subtracted — not fulfilling it is just a gap, an "I haven't done it yet."
- The output of the exercise: lots of arrows from your current activities back to your values builds confidence that what you're doing today is genuinely fed by who you are. Or it reveals you should change — but as running *toward* something, not *away*.
- Changing major / quitting a job matters far less than knowing **why** you're doing what you're doing.
