## Overview

David delivers a comprehensive introduction to expanded PVC (foamed PVC sheet) — a material he considers best-in-class for prototyping panels but underserved by online documentation because no single industry champions it. The 6 mm (~1/4 inch) thickness is the hub: it matches heat-set insert depths, aluminum extrusion slots, panel screw lengths, and woodworking standards simultaneously. He demonstrates cutting, drilling, lossless knife cutting, edge truing, bonding to wood/ABS/itself, and applying vinyl wraps for finish.

## Key takeaways

- 6 mm (or near-1/4 in) is the sweet-spot thickness — interoperates with wood-panel screws, 2020/3030 extrusion slots, heat-set inserts, and L-screws.
- Expanded PVC accepts wood screws without pre-drilling, will not crack like acrylic, will not melt under saws like polycarbonate, and is fully waterproof unlike MDF/plywood.
- A utility knife with a sharp blade gives true zero-kerf cuts — eliminates kerf-accounting math when laying out multiple parts on one sheet.
- Long ruler beats short carpenter's square for visually setting perpendicularity — eyeballed gap match across a longer reference reaches sub-degree accuracy.
- Bonding requires air access to the joint: large flat-to-flat bonds may need 24+ hours, narrow contact bands cure in ~30 min.
- PVC primer + clear PVC cement bonds expanded PVC to wood, MDF, painted steel, and ABS reliably; CA glue handles fine smooth joints; construction adhesive bridges large gaps.
- Vinyl wraps with vented adhesive backing are David's preferred finish — paint adheres better to PVC than to nylon/HDPE but vinyl is cleaner.
