# More than you ever wanted to know about tape

## Overview

A 70-minute reference catalog of ~20 tape varieties used in mechanical engineering and mechatronics. David walks through each tape on-camera — touch, feel, applications, failure modes, cost — arguing that "tape for almost every application" beats duct tape's one-size-fits-all reputation. Explicitly framed as a reference to jump around in, not watch linearly.

## Key takeaways

- **Duct tape is nearly useless** for any purpose other than wrapping sharp utility-blade trash. For almost every job there's a specific tape that outperforms it.
- **[[concepts/emissivity|Emissivity]] ruins infrared temperature measurements** on shiny or transparent surfaces. Cover the target with plain beige masking tape before measuring, and trust only relative readings between two tape-covered surfaces.
- **Cheap brands of [[entities/materials/kapton-tape|Kapton tape]]** (~$8 on Amazon) are chemically the same polymer as data-sheet-certified rolls (~$30) but skip the qualification paperwork.
- **[[entities/materials/ptfe-teflon|PTFE / Teflon]] pipe thread tape** only seals tapered threads — compression fittings rely on O-rings and don't need tape.
- **Double-sided foam tape** behaves like a spring for [[concepts/vibration-damping|vibration damping]]: doubling thickness halves the spring constant; doubling bonded surface area halves deflection under the same load. Useful for killing "drum" resonance in thin-walled enclosures.

## Techniques demonstrated

- [[concepts/emissivity|Emissivity compensation for IR temperature]] — cover target in matte beige masking tape before reading with a thermal camera.
- [[concepts/vibration-damping|Vibration damping]] — apply weighted foam-tape sandwich inside thin-walled enclosures.
- Thermocouple mounting with [[entities/materials/kapton-tape|Kapton]] + fiberglass sandwich for accurate surface temperature (avoid measuring air instead of substrate).
- Marking round stock for a perpendicular or 45° cut by wrapping non-stretch tape and using it as a sight-line.
- Preventing composite/splintered edges on circular-saw cuts by taping the cut line first.
