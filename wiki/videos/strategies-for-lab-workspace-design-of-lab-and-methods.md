## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] shares a draft video walking through how he sets up and continuously improves a multidisciplinary mechatronics lab. He covers labeling discipline, drawer organization, when 3D printing is and isn't worthwhile, mounting strategies for wall panels, surface choices, and the [[concepts/5s-methodology|5S]]/[[concepts/kaizen|kaizen]] thinking carried over from his Toyota background. Self-described as a rough draft — he treats it as a feedback loop rather than a polished tutorial.

## Key takeaways

- Always trim labels with scissors — sloppy labels hanging off edges peel and create disorder; the time spent now compounds.
- Standardization is 5S step 4: a designated home for every tool, with the most common tool in a drawer labeled and others self-organizing by spatial inference.
- Enclosed storage (drawers) beats open hanging storage for cleanliness and category integrity.
- Decide whether to 3D print or use a simpler solution (scissors-cut foam, bicycle-rubber bands) — three CAD versions of a print is a nuisance signal.
- Surface color matters: blonde wood shows debris and forces cleaning; debris-hostile surfaces enforce hygiene.
- Mounting choices on painted walls should anticipate future moves — minimize hole patterns and repaints.
- Scalability of decisions: "Jeff Bezos would make the same call" — when something breaks, your replacement plan must already exist.
