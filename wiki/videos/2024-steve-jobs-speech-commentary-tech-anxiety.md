## Overview

A 57-minute podcast-format unpacking of Steve Jobs' 2005 Stanford commencement speech, with [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] anchoring each Jobs theme — connecting the dots, mortality as a decision tool, finding what you love, ignoring the AI hype-cycle — to specific autobiographical turns: leaving [[entities/brands/toyota|Toyota]] in 2014, the Master's at [[entities/brands/texas-am|Texas A&M]], a tragic period overseas with his ex-wife, and the present life of "a dozen missions at the same time." The framing is "treating the audience as a younger brother" who needs concrete examples that pursuing-your-passion advice isn't a fairy tale.

## Key takeaways

- You can only connect the dots looking backward — old journals and photographs become a fixed point that today's noise can't move.
- Quitting Toyota (a "lifelong job") was driven by an internal sense of not making the impact he could.
- A career break — even a year overseas — does not destroy you, and removing that fear unlocks decisions driven by free will rather than safety nets.
- "Race against no one": when your path is uniquely yours, you have no deadlines competing with peers.
- Use mortality as a daily filter for the *spare* portion of your time — not for whether to pay your electric bill or quit the law, but for which direction to push within constraints.
- "Too many days in a row" of "no" — that's when bigger reorientation is warranted, not single bad days.
- 10 years of accumulated, personally-owned notes at [[entities/places/texas-am|Texas A&M]] (no IP-assignment clause, no non-compete) function like 10 engineers on his team.
