## Overview

A close-up tutorial on [[entities/tools/dupont-connector|DuPont 2.54 mm connectors]] - the signal-wire workhorse of robotics. [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] dissects the connector into three sections (A: insulation grip, B: conductor grip, C: mating area), shows the matching front die on the [[entities/tools/dupont-crimper|DuPont crimper]], and demonstrates the strip-then-trim technique that avoids losing strands of fragile 22 AWG wire.

## Key takeaways

- DuPont connectors carry signal, not power - 22 AWG conductor can't handle real current.
- The crimper's front die has two compression sections separated by a shelf; the connector hangs on that shelf during alignment.
- Connector anatomy: section A (big arms) grabs insulation; section B (small arms) grabs conductor; section C must stay clear because that's where mating happens.
- Strip 4 mm: ~2 mm into section A, 4 mm reaching through B, leaving section C empty.
- Don't use generic strippers on 22 AWG stranded - they tear strands. Strip more than needed, then snip back to length with [[entities/tools/flush-cutters|dikes]].
- Twist the conductor gently after stripping so strands stay grouped on insertion.
- Verify after crimping: insulation in front, conductor in the middle, nothing in the back.
