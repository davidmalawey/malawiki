# Label supplies to multiply results

## Overview

A 24-minute labs-and-organization philosophy video. David argues that labels — even simple colored tape dots — pay back their cost many times over in time saved, inventory visibility, and passive training of team members. Rooted in [[concepts/5s-methodology|5S]] from his first Toyota internship (which he didn't "get" for years), now applied to his lab at [[entities/brands/texas-am|Texas A&M]].

## Key takeaways

- **[[concepts/5s-methodology|5S]] is not obvious until you've managed enough people** to realize they need to act without verbal communication. A label that says "chair" on a chair isn't a joke — it's what tells a non-English-speaking night-shift worker where the chair belongs.
- **Start with color, not text.** Green tape on a pen, green tape on the pen's home slot — the lab organizes itself without anyone being asked to reorganize.
- **Orders leave a timestamp for free.** Blue dots = ordered 2023, green dots = ordered 2024. No separate expiration log needed.
- **[[concepts/calibrate-the-humans|Calibrate the humans]]** — write measured values on a label even when no one is asked to read them. Passers-by absorb "5 m/s feels like *this*" subconsciously.
- **Outdated labels are data, not trash.** Old container label tells you what substance was in it, whether it needs cleaning, whether there are residual fumes. Leave it on until disposal.
- **QR codes > printed PDFs for manuals.** Use a service that lets you replace the linked file without reprinting the label; generate QR codes *before* populating the link so labels can be placed early.
- **Manufacturers leave traces on their labels.** Copyright dates, safety-standard references, and product manuals contain information that the marketing team scrubs from the website. A $4 filter listed in a $20 vacuum's compatibility manual is "gold."

## Techniques demonstrated

- [[concepts/5s-methodology|5S labeling]] — handwritten first, printed only once utility is proven.
- Color-coding with circular dot labels as a 2D index (color × year).
- Printing simple [[concepts/parametric-design|parametric]] 3D gauges (e.g. an M6-size check block) for shop-floor sorting without calipers.
- Placing labels on one face of a box only, so a backwards-facing box is itself a visible error signal.
- Removing [[entities/tools/sharpie|Sharpie]] from plastic with [[entities/materials/isopropyl-alcohol|isopropyl alcohol]] — first of the "four horsemen of solvents."
- Using QR codes as indirect references so content can be swapped server-side without re-labeling.
