## Overview

David tests new black [[entities/materials/hot-melt-glue|hot-melt glue]] sticks for insulating exposed pins and short-circuit-prone contacts on his SCUTTLE electronics. The mechanical message: black glue gives opaque, high-contrast coverage so damage and gaps are visible at a glance — a free [[concepts/5s-methodology|5S]]-style upgrade over translucent white glue. He also reuses his heat-shrink heat gun to re-flow and smooth applied beads.

## Key takeaways

- Hot glue is reversible — peels off cleanly when you want to redo a board, unlike epoxy.
- Black sticks appear to run a hotter melting point than white; mind the plastic substrate when re-flowing.
- Re-melt with the heat-shrink heat gun to flatten beads and remove stringing; let parts cool unloaded so they don't warp.
- Color is a free communication channel: black = "this covers electronics," white = something else. Every free upgrade in a many-thousand-part system is worth taking.
- This is [[concepts/5s-methodology|5S]] thinking: opaque coverage makes damage visible the instant a robot is dropped or stressed.
