## Overview

[[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] glues the v1.0 3D-printed [[entities/tools/wheel-pulley|wheel pulley]] (ABS) into the inside dish of a [[entities/materials/skateboard-wheel|skateboard wheel]] using [[entities/materials/super-glue|super glue]]. He explains why pulley side matters (bending moment on the axle bolt), how to verify the deeper-dish side with [[entities/tools/calipers|calipers]], and how to seat the spacer with a tiny dab of glue so it doesn't tumble during bearing press-in. Cyanoacrylate is contact-mating only - it does not fill gaps.

## Key takeaways

- Wheels have a deeper outer dish and shallower inner dish; orientation determines bearing-center distance, which sets the bending moment on the axle bolt.
- Wheel measurement check: lay something flat across the rubber and measure depth with calipers - example readings 15-16 mm outer vs 13.5 mm inner.
- The internal spacer between the two bearings tumbles freely until both bearings are pressed in - dab super glue to keep it located. It will work loose later from vibration, which is fine.
- The pulley mounts on the inside (shallow-dish) face. Verify the printed teeth mate cleanly with the [[entities/tools/htd5-belt|HTD5 belt]].
- The three pulley arms must contact the wheel walls with friction, no looseness.
- Apply glue on the outside face of each arm and a thin bead around the rim border.
- Super glue bottles are mostly air; tilt down and let air rise, then squeeze gently.
- Cyanoacrylate is for surfaces in contact - it doesn't fill gaps. Don't over-apply.
- Weight the assembly for a couple of minutes while it cures.
