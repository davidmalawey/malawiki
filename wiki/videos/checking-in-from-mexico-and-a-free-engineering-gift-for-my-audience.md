## Overview

A travel-flavored value essay. [[entities/people/david-malawey|David]] reports back from Cancun, where a 90-minute timeshare pitch revealed that the upgrade he actually wanted — venting the bathroom so the shower doesn't smell like the toilet — was priced higher than even the million-dollar package. His "free gift" to the audience is the [[entities/tools/inner-lights-timer-switch|Inner Lights timer switch]] (about $22) that solves the same problem with a 4-wire retrofit, plus a hand-drawn wiring diagram packaged as a PDF.

## Key takeaways

- Real engineering value is often a $22 part plus the knowledge to install it — and that combination beats six-figure package add-ons in actual quality of life.
- The bathroom-vent timer switch is a drop-in replacement for an ordinary wall switch: black to line, red to switched load, white to neutral, ground to ground.
- Visiting [[entities/places/home-depot-cancun|Home Depot Cancun]] revealed that Mexican hardware is sold predominantly in inches/ounces/pounds, the gas-fittings aisle is busy (more DIY propane installs), and many stamped-steel boxes are made in Mexico/China and often thinner than US equivalents.
- Open-source hardware can stay imperial-units-friendly when the cheapest off-the-shelf component is rated in imperial — sister metric versions can be added later by the community.
- "Things are free now" — David's continuing thesis that high-leverage everyday improvements cost almost nothing and are mostly limited by knowledge distribution, not money.
